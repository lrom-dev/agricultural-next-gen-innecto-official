**ABSTRACT**

The Philippines continues to thrive off of agriculture to sustain its daily economic and nutritional needs. In fact, Mindanao — the Philippines’ food basket — supplies 45% of the country’s food requirements. However, despite supplying the majority of the country’s produce, the island faces a lower value chain, affecting the livelihoods of farmers. As a result, the sector heavily relies on middlemen, causing market self-inflation and lower bargaining power for farmers. Leading factors include fragmented governance and negligible support, and a lack of technical preparedness. AI and IoT-automated irrigation aims to modernize the agricultural sector, which could boost the value of Mindanao’s produce. However, the island’s sector is largely disconnected, calling for the need to connect the island digitally. According to the results, ANI: Agricultural Next-gen Innecto, can solve this problem by encouraging direct communication between consumers and producers alike. With a median FCP of 0.6s, and a median LCP of 0.7s, ANI is a light-weight application that loads in fast on PCs. However, before launching ANI, the researcher suggests further development to include mobile devices and real-time communication. As it stands, ANI is only a static locally-hosted web app. Only by further developing ANI can it serve its purpose to the people of Mindanao.

***CONCLUSIONS***

Based on these findings, it can be concluded that ANI: Agricultural Next-gen Innecto has a highly significant potential in addressing Mindanao’s agricultural situation through digital solutions. Specifically, by connecting consumers and producers directly while encouraging professional networking and research, middlemen reliance can be effectively eliminated. It runs smoothly on PC devices. However, to be able to reach a broader audience, it must first be developed to mitigate its mobile optimization bottlenecks. According to the GLM and the Wilcoxon Signed-Rank tests, device type is a major factor in ANI’s performance (p <.001). However, ANI successfully rejects 4 out of 15 null hypotheses (H01b, H04a, H04b, H05a), indicating a significant difference between Beta and Omega for FCP, Alpha and Beta for TBT, Beta and Omega for TBT, and Alpha and Beta for CLS. Therefore, this study suggests further developing ANI to include mobile users, as well as to include qualitative feedback from real subjects to gain invaluable real-life insights. Furthermore, ANI should be backed up by a policy engaging the digital divide and technical literacy skills among the sector because the biggest challenge for implementing ANI is the digital divide.
